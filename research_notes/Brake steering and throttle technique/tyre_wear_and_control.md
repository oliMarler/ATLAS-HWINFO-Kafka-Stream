## Mechanisms: how sliding, slip angle/slip ratio, wheelspin, lockups, kerbs and overdriving create heat and wear; front- vs rear-limited tracks; the "tyre is a consumable" mindset; graining, blistering, overheating vs underheating

### Takeaway
Heat and wear come from slip beyond what the corner needs: too much steering (scrub), wheelspin, lockups and "overdriving" all put energy into the tread without adding speed, and the tyre "goes off" sooner. Graining and blistering need opposite cures (work the tyre harder vs cool it), but sources disagree on whether graining is a cold or hot effect. In the current iRacing model (2025 onward), small slides and spins are punished less than before, but sustained pushing costs more wear than in earlier seasons.

### Cited Findings
**Slip angle and steering (scrub)**
- [PRO] Turning the wheel too quickly gets to steering lock before the tyres' slip angles catch up; the driver then over-steers, which "scrubs" the tyres, builds unnecessary heat and accelerates tread wear. Steering should be precise and smooth, turned only as much as the corner needs. Don Alexander, NASA Speed News, 2014 (club/pro driving coach). — [NASA Speed News](https://nasaspeed.news/tech/suspension/tire-management-how-to-get-the-most-out-of-your-race-tires-from-behind-the-wheel/)
- [PRO] Same author gives peak traction at a slip angle of about 6 degrees, with "very near maximum traction actually occurs between 4.5 and 5.5 degrees"; running slightly below peak slip angle keeps grip in reserve and saves tyres: "Great drivers drive at the limits of traction at smaller slip angles." (single source; generic race tyres, not GTP-specific numbers) — [NASA Speed News](https://nasaspeed.news/tech/suspension/tire-management-how-to-get-the-most-out-of-your-race-tires-from-behind-the-wheel/)
- [SIM] Coach Dave Academy on iRacing: "Less aggressive steering inputs" help control temperatures and reduce wear; excessive steering causes unnecessary scrubbing, which raises pressures. — [Coach Dave Academy, Understanding tire pressures in iRacing](https://coachdaveacademy.com/tutorials/understanding-tire-pressures-in-iracing/)

**Wheelspin (slip ratio, drive) and lockups (slip ratio, braking)**
- [PRO] "Too hard and fast on the throttle can induce wheel-spin," which reduces traction, overheats the tyres and speeds wear; hard acceleration heats the rears on rear-drive cars; hard braking heats the fronts quickly; stabbing the brakes can lock the wheels, damaging the tyres and overheating the tread. — [NASA Speed News](https://nasaspeed.news/tech/suspension/tire-management-how-to-get-the-most-out-of-your-race-tires-from-behind-the-wheel/)
- [PRO] Connor De Phillippi (BMW GTP), 2023: GTP cars have more power, slower tyre warm-up and less downforce than the old DPi ("the tire takes a lot longer to come up to speed and we have less downforce"); he recalled wheelspin in fourth and fifth gear out of Turn 8 at Long Beach and expected the car to be "a handful on older tires." — [Motorsport.com](https://www.motorsport.com/imsa/news/gtp-tire-challenges-allow-drivers-to-make-a-difference/10467756/)
- [SIM] iRacing GT3 tyre model update (Season 3 2025): avoid lock-ups, wheelspin and overdriving because they accelerate wear; be smooth with brakes, steering "and especially throttle", since slight excess throttle can snap the car. — [Coach Dave Academy, 2025 iRacing GT3 tyre model update explained](https://coachdaveacademy.com/announcements/the-2025-iracing-gt3-tyre-model-update-explained/)
- [SIM] Brake lock-ups and spins can flat-spot a fresh set (especially without ABS); avoiding lock-ups, big slides and spins "strongly" affects tyre life and can force an early stop. — [Fanatec, Tire Management in Endurance Sim Racing](https://www.fanatec.com/uk/en/explorer/games/gaming-tips/tire-management-in-endurance-sim-racing/)

**Overdriving**
- [PRO] Accelerated wear has two main causes: a poor setup, and overdriving the tyres (excess heat), the second depending entirely on how the driver uses the controls; a driver who turns too much "will not be as fast after five or 10 laps." — [NASA Speed News](https://nasaspeed.news/tech/suspension/tire-management-how-to-get-the-most-out-of-your-race-tires-from-behind-the-wheel/)

**Throttle-steer to spare a specific tyre**
- [PRO] Brief lifts or added throttle shift load between tyres and reduce the steering needed; Alexander used this at Willow Springs Turn 2 to cut steering lock and protect the left-front. (single source) — [NASA Speed News](https://nasaspeed.news/tech/suspension/tire-management-how-to-get-the-most-out-of-your-race-tires-from-behind-the-wheel/)

**"Tyre is a consumable" mindset**
- [PRO] De Phillippi: "Obviously you have to double-stint some tires but you want to avoid being on those tires as long as you can"; worn tyres give drivers "some kinda scope to make a difference as far as how we drive, how we manage a stint" — "It's not just a 'flat-out from beginning to end'- type deal." — [Motorsport.com](https://www.motorsport.com/imsa/news/gtp-tire-challenges-allow-drivers-to-make-a-difference/10467756/)
- [SIM] In endurance racing never go past the tyre's grip limit; if you do in a close fight, "compensate for that tire life lost at a later stage of your stint"; "never operate above the tire temperature window." — [Fanatec](https://www.fanatec.com/uk/en/explorer/games/gaming-tips/tire-management-in-endurance-sim-racing/)
- [SIM] iRacing GT3 update: pushing harder gives more performance but "at the cost of increased wear"; wear is "more track- and stint-dependent" and higher than in previous seasons; expect a noticeable grip loss late in each stint; conservation matters more on hot tracks, less on cool ones. — [Coach Dave Academy](https://coachdaveacademy.com/announcements/the-2025-iracing-gt3-tyre-model-update-explained/)

**iRacing-specific: sliding is punished less, wear more (2025 model)**
- [SIM] Announced changes (paraphrased by Coach Dave): "Reduced temperature sensitivity and more resilience to sliding with better grip recovery"; small slides no longer cause a sharp loss of grip; spins should no longer ruin a set for the rest of a stint; "Improved stiffness (softer vertical, stiffer lateral)." Coach Dave says a GTP tyre update came "a few months" earlier and the GT3 change will "likely be similar". — [Coach Dave Academy](https://coachdaveacademy.com/announcements/the-2025-iracing-gt3-tyre-model-update-explained/)
- [SIM] iRacing 2025 Season 2 release notes (season start 18 March 2025): "Vehicle engineers completed updates to the GTP Class Cars and the LMP2 tires, providing substantial improvements to the changes from 2025 Season 1." (search summary; page returned 403) — [SteamDB patch notes](https://steamdb.info/patchnotes/17683273); Fanatec preview says the earlier model "didn't quite hit the mark" for cornering and the fix uses real-world data for high-speed corners (search summary) — [Fanatec, iRacing updates 2025](https://www.fanatec.com/uk/en/explorer/games/iracing/iracing-updates-2025/)
- [SIM] Box This Lap (Cadillac V-Series.R, after the Season 2 2025 update): degradation is felt in the car's behaviour rather than only as a number; understeer and oversteer build progressively rather than suddenly; pressure changes have a "tangible impact" on handling and longevity. (single reviewer, subjective) — [Box This Lap](https://boxthislap.org/iracing-this-time-the-tyres-feel-alive/)

**Graining, blistering, over/underheating**
- [PRO, F1 explainers] Graining is a surface failure: the tread tears into ribbons/ridges, the effective contact patch shrinks, grip falls, which invites more sliding and more graining. Blistering is an internal effect from too much heat (gas lifts the tread). A graining tyre wants to be worked harder; a blistering tyre wants to be cooled; misdiagnosis makes it worse fast. (search summary of fan/explainer sites) — [F1-Fansite glossary](https://f1-fansite.com/nl/glossary/graining/); [Flow Racers](https://flowracers.com/blog/graining-and-blistering-in-f1/); [Motorsport Explained](https://motorsportexplained.com/graining-f1-tyres/)
- **Conflict:** one explainer says graining comes from rubber overheated by sliding; another says it comes from a compound that is too cold and slides too much. Not resolved here. — same sources as above (search summary)
- [PRO] Pirelli (GT World Challenge Europe, Imola 2021): cars running near the bottom of the operating window had more trouble on restarts after safety cars, while long-stint degradation was not a problem — i.e. underheating shows up at restarts. (search summary) — [Sportscar365](https://sportscar365.com/?p=105144)

**Front-limited vs rear-limited**
- [PRO, F1, dated] Pat Symonds (Renault, 2005) called Hockenheim a "rear-limited" circuit because it is mostly hard acceleration out of slow and medium corners, making rear tyre performance critical. (search summary; old and F1) — [Tyrepress](https://tyrepress.com/2005/07/michelin-f1-tyres-the-answer)
- No authoritative general explainer of "front-limited" tracks was found (only a forum thread). — [F1Technical forum](https://www.f1technical.net/forum/viewtopic.php?p=1075866)

**Kerbs**

### Inferences
- The common mechanism across all sources is "slip energy that does not buy speed": excess steering angle (lateral slip), wheelspin (longitudinal slip under drive), lockup (longitudinal slip under braking) and overdriving (operating past peak slip). A coaching assistant can treat each as a measurable symptom: steering angle above the lap's reference for the same corner speed, rear wheel speed exceeding front, front wheel speed dropping below vehicle speed under braking. (inferred)
- GTP cars are torque-heavy, hybrid, relatively low-downforce and slow to warm (De Phillippi), so rear-tyre wear from traction zones is the likely limiter at stop-start street/slow-corner tracks, and front wear at high-speed, long-corner tracks. This is an extrapolation from the rear-limited definition and the GTP characteristics; no source assigns IMSA tracks to front- or rear-limited categories. (inferred)
- Because the 2025 iRacing model recovers from small slides, the penalty for an occasional moment is lower than it used to be; the penalty for a stint of habitual extra slip (wear) is higher. Coaching emphasis should therefore be on repeatable, systematic slip (every lap, same corner) rather than one-off moments. (inferred)

## Reading the tyres: temperatures across the tread, hot pressure targets, wear readings, lap-time fall-off, and how engineers and drivers use them during a stint

### Takeaway
Read the three-point tread temperatures as two separate questions: middle vs edges says pressure (hot middle = over-inflated, cool middle = under-inflated), inner vs outer says camber (inner a little hotter is normal with negative camber). In iRacing, garage/pit temps are snapshots that are cooled by the pit lane and skewed by the last corners, so read them with care; telemetry channels exist for surface temps, carcass temps, pressures and percent tread left per zone. Real-world pros run to hot-pressure targets set by the tyre maker (Michelin raised the Hypercar minimum hot pressure to 2.1 bar at Le Mans 2026; Pirelli GT3 target 2.00 bar hot).

### Cited Findings
**Three-point temperatures: what they mean**
- [SIM] iRacing shows O (outer), M (middle), I (inner) per tyre (12 readings on a GT3). Target: middle about equal to inner and outer; cooler middle usually means pressure too low, hotter middle means too high. Camber adjustment evens temps when the inside reads hotter than the outside. Over-inflation rounds the tread centre and shrinks the contact patch; under-inflation runs the tyre on its edges, raising friction, temperature and wear. — [Coach Dave Academy](https://coachdaveacademy.com/tutorials/understanding-tire-pressures-in-iracing/)
- [SIM] Forum rule of thumb: inner and outer should differ by about 6-10 degrees, inner hotter; camber controls that spread and pressure controls the middle. (forum, not official) — [Overtake.gg thread](https://www.overtake.gg/threads/question-about-tyre-temp.80015/)
- [SIM] A setup site says inner should be the hottest region but never more than about 7°C above the outer, and gives an iRacing optimum of 85-105°C. (search summary; low authority; conflicts slightly with the forum 6-10 degree figure, and units of the forum figure are unstated) — [SimRacingSetup](https://simracingsetup.com/iracing/iracing-tyre-setup-guide/)

**Caveats on pit/garage readings (iRacing)**
- [SIM] iRacing's own blog (2010, old model): temperature is "highly transient and tends to spike as the result of high corning loads," so the reading depends on what the tyre did just before leaving the track; a slow pit-lane run cools the tyre and can reflect straight-line camber; lock-ups, spins, or brushing a wall/car make end-of-run data unreliable; suspension damage voids readings. (dated, but the reading-method caution still applies) — [iRacing, "Hot and tired"](https://www.iracing.com/hot-and-tired/)
- [SIM] "For most cars, iRacing doesn't give you real-time tyre information while driving, so you need to pit to get this data." — [Coach Dave Academy](https://coachdaveacademy.com/tutorials/understanding-tire-pressures-in-iracing/)
- [SIM] A 2022 user post says there is no live tyre-wear indicator; percent remaining is only visible in the garage afterwards. (search summary, user-level, possibly outdated) — [Steam community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/266410/discussions/0/3723945543090827600)

**iRacing telemetry channels (for an assistant reading the SDK/IBT)**
- [SIM] AIM's iRacing telemetry guide (PDF dated Sept 2020) lists per corner: `LFtempL/M/R` "surface temp" (left/middle/right), `LFtempCL/CM/CR` "carcass temp", `LFwearL/M/R` "pct tread left", `LFpressure`, `LFcoldPressure`, plus pit-service pressure channels (`PitSvLFP` etc.), `TrackTemp` and `TrackTempCrew`; same pattern for RF/LR/RR. The guide does not say which channels update live vs only in the pit stall. — [AIM iRacing 102 guide (PDF)](https://store.aimsports.com/download/doc/eng/simracing/iRacing_102_eng.pdf)

**Temperature reference values**
- [SIM] Coach Dave iRacing GT3 baseline: cold pressures at the minimum, hot pressures 1.50-1.55 bar, hot tyre temperatures 80-90°C on track. The same article notes very low pressures can work on GT3 and the model keeps changing. GT3 only, no GTP figures given. — [Coach Dave Academy](https://coachdaveacademy.com/tutorials/understanding-tire-pressures-in-iracing/); also [Coach Dave iRacing FAQ](https://coachdaveacademy.com/release-notes/iracing-faq/) (search summary)
- [PRO] Generic race-tyre contact-patch optimum "between 160 degree and 220 degrees Fahrenheit" (about 71-104°C). (single source, generic, 2014) — [NASA Speed News](https://nasaspeed.news/tech/suspension/tire-management-how-to-get-the-most-out-of-your-race-tires-from-behind-the-wheel/)
- [PRO] Michelin Hypercar compounds are chosen by **track** temperature (not tyre temperature): soft about 0-15°C (dry or slightly damp), medium 15-25°C, hard above 25°C plus abrasive surfaces. — [Michelin UK](https://www.michelin.co.uk/why-michelin/motorsport/michelin-as-wec-exclusive-tyre-supplier-for-hypercar). **Conflict:** an older Michelin Australia page puts the hard above 30°C and the soft below 15°C or night-time. (search summary) — [Michelin Australia](https://www.michelin.com.au/why-michelin/motorsport/michelin-exclusive-tyre-supplier-for-the-wec-s-hypercar-class)

**Hot pressure targets (real world)**
- [PRO] Le Mans 2026: Michelin raised minimum hot tyre pressures for Hypercar from 2.0 bar (29 psi) to 2.1 bar (30.5 psi) after the pre-test; race-day track temperatures were expected around 50°C, 15-20°C above practice. (single source) — [Motorsport.com long-run analysis](https://www.motorsport.com/lemans/news/le-mans-24-long-run-analysis-where-the-hypercars-stand-before-the-race/10829713/)
- [PRO] Pirelli GT3 (GT World Challenge Asia 2022 and 2023 tech bulletins): hot target 2.00 bar, minimum inflation 1.40 bar before running. (search summary of PDFs) — [GTWC Asia 2023 bulletin](https://www.gt-world-challenge-asia.com/documents/notice/133/GTWCAsia23_01+TechBulletin.pdf); Porsche Carrera Cup North America 2026 (Pirelli slicks, not GT3): hot 2.00 bar/29.0 psi, minimum cold 1.25 bar. (search summary) — [IMSA Carrera Cup NA P-Book 2026](https://www.imsa.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/32/2026/03/03/P_Book-Porsche-Carrera-Cup-North-America-2026-V1.1.pdf)
- No Michelin GTD/GT3 or GTP (IMSA) hot-pressure figure was found.

**How pros use the data during a stint**
- [PRO] LMP2 (Goodyear): tyre temperature and pressure plus driver feedback are the main stint-performance inputs; driver Gabriel Aubry calls it a "triangulation between driver, team engineer and Goodyear". (captured by sibling researcher) — [PMW Magazine](https://pmw-magazine.com/news/team-news/goodyear-reveals-how-driver-tire-engineer-relationship-enhances-performance.html); [Goodyear Europe](https://news.goodyear.eu/goodyear-at-le-mans-the-insight-into-a-driver-tire-engineer-relationship/)
- [PRO] Michelin's development testing for the 2026 Hypercar/GTP tyre: runs started and ended on a reference tyre; short runs of about 7-8 laps, long runs of 15 laps on one set; drivers rated grip and balance on a -3 to +3 grid, then the team cross-checked with data. — [Sportscar365 (Substack)](https://sportscar365.substack.com/p/insight-how-michelin-developed-its)
- [PRO, F1] Mercedes' Andrew Shovlin: give information when the driver can act on it, e.g. tyre temperatures mid out-lap. (captured by sibling researcher) — [Autosport](https://www.autosport.com/f1/news/the-secrets-of-in-race-radio-chat-5102490/5102490/)

### Inferences
- iRacing telemetry uses **left/middle/right** naming, while the garage shows **outer/middle/inner**. Geometrically, on the left-side tyres "L" is the outer edge and "R" the inner; on the right-side tyres "L" is the inner edge and "R" the outer. An assistant must remap per side before applying "inner should be slightly hotter". (inferred from channel names; not confirmed by iRacing documentation)
- Because pit readings are transient and cooled by the pit lane, compare like with like: same lap of the stint, same in-lap pattern (no cool-down lap), and read the spread (I-M-O differences) rather than absolute values. Carcass channels, if they update live, are a steadier indicator of the tyre's working temperature than surface channels. (inferred; live-update behaviour unconfirmed)
- `LFwearL/M/R` as "pct tread left" lets an assistant estimate wear rate per lap and per zone (e.g., an outer-edge wear rate higher than inner suggests too little camber or a lot of scrub). Wear rate per lap times laps remaining against the double-stint decision is a natural use. (inferred)
- Lap-time fall-off should be judged fuel-corrected: as fuel burns the car gets lighter and faster, which hides tyre loss. A flat lap time over a stint on a burning fuel load already means the tyres are losing time. (inferred; no source quantified fuel effect per lap for GTP)

- No official iRacing statement found on which tyre channels update live in GTP cars versus only in the pit stall, or on recommended hot pressures/temperatures for the iRacing GTP cars (Cadillac, BMW, Porsche, Acura); no iRacing GTP car manual text on tyres was reachable in this search.
- No published lap-time fall-off per lap for GTP, LMP2 or GT3 (see Quantitative).

## Managing a stint: adapting as grip falls, attack vs protect, double-stinting in GTP, out-lap and post-caution warm-up, cold-tyre pace, and iRacing-specific advice

### Takeaway
Real GTP stints are shaped by limited tyre sets and slow warm-up: teams double- (and now triple-) stint tyres, drivers manage rather than run flat out, and the most dangerous moments are out-laps and restarts on cold tyres. In iRacing's current model tyres also start cool and take several laps to peak (developers' estimate: 4th-5th flying lap, stated for GT3), so out-laps and restarts need margin under braking and gentle throttle, and burnouts/weaving are poor warm-up tools.

### Cited Findings
**Double- and multi-stinting (real world)**
- [PRO] IMSA GTP (2023): teams had about 21 sets for a race with stints of about 50 minutes under green, so up to eight sets had to do two stints; Michelin's Hans Emmel expected varied approaches, including double-stinting one side of the car at a time, or removing a set and refitting it later. (search summary; attribution between Racer and Sportscar365 not confirmed) — [Racer](https://racer.com/2023/01/28/insight-alternate-strategies-and-new-thinking-for-gtp-race-plans/)
- [PRO] Rolex 24 2023: GTP limited to 25 sets for the event and 20 for the race; Sportscar365 said regular double-stinting had not been seen in the top class in the championship's ten-year history. (search summary) — [Sportscar365](https://sportscar365.com/?p=112047)
- [PRO] Michelin says the 2026 Pilot Sport Endurance can handle triple or quadruple stints; double-stinting a full set is considered ideal for WEC six-hour races. — [Sportscar365 (Substack)](https://sportscar365.substack.com/p/insight-how-michelin-developed-its)
- [PRO] Le Mans 2026: Michelin's Pierre Alves said triple and quadruple stints were completed "on a regular basis"; the #8 Toyota ran a quadruple stint on one set of softs; one stint covered "more than 600km on a single set". — [Michelin Motorsport](https://news.motorsport.michelin.com/en/articles/victory-and-more-records-for-michelin-at-le-mans). Pre-race, Motorsport.com judged triples necessary to win and quadruples "highly unlikely". — [Motorsport.com](https://www.motorsport.com/lemans/news/le-mans-24-long-run-analysis-where-the-hypercars-stand-before-the-race/10829713/)
- [PRO] Autosport (Le Mans test 2026): quadruple stints were normal in LMP1; with heavier Hypercars triples became the norm; Michelin aimed to widen the overlap between compounds. (search summary) — [Autosport](https://autosport.com/wec/news/what-we-learned-from-the-le-mans-24-hours-test/10828542/)
- [PRO] LMP2 (Goodyear): at COTA (2019/20) WEC allowed 18 tyres across qualifying and a six-hour race, making double-stinting central; Goodyear's Mike McGregor: "We saw consistent performance throughout each stint." At Le Mans 2022 two LMP2 cars ran five stints on one set overnight without extreme degradation (up to 750 km per set); da Costa: "Le Mans is more of a low degradation track, so we can do more stints with the tyres." (search summary; which Goodyear page holds which quote not confirmed) — [Goodyear, Double Texas podium](https://news.goodyear.eu/double-texas-podium-for-goodyear-teams/); [Goodyear, speed and reliability of modern LMP2](https://news.goodyear.eu/goodyear-hails-teams-for-the-speed-and-reliability-of-modern-lmp2/)
- [PRO] GTD (Michelin, Sebring 2024): Michelin North America's Jason Anzalone described the GTD tyre as "the Michelin Pilot Sport Pro with double stint capable technology". — [Michelin Motorsport](https://news.motorsport.michelin.com/en/articles/imsa-at-sebring-delivers-thrills-and-strong-performance-from-manufacturers-teams-and-michelin-tires)
- [PRO] WEC Bahrain (Toyota): technical director Pascal Vasselon said the team was on the "knife's edge" with degradation, the #8 had very big degradation, and a different tyre strategy helped; Mike Conway said they had never taken a set that far into a double stint in practice. (search summary; exact Sportscar365 page and year not confirmed) — [Sportscar365](https://sportscar365.com/lemans/wec/toyota-in-control-at-halfway-in-6h-bahrain/)

**Track character**
- [PRO] De Phillippi expected Laguna Seca's abrasive surface to make tyre timing "a lottery as to who takes that hit early, who takes the hit late," and said "Laguna puts a lot more energy through the tires, so the warm-up phase will be so different from Long Beach." — [Motorsport.com](https://www.motorsport.com/imsa/news/gtp-tire-challenges-allow-drivers-to-make-a-difference/10467756/)

**Cold tyres: out-laps and restarts (real world)**
- [PRO] De Phillippi: at Long Beach 2023 qualifying the tyres took roughly 11-13 laps to fully come in. — [Motorsport.com](https://www.motorsport.com/imsa/news/gtp-tire-challenges-allow-drivers-to-make-a-difference/10467756/)
- [PRO] Racer (2023): GTP cars are "an absolute handful" on cold tyres; drivers blame the weight for poor control out of the window; cars left the pits on cold tyres and were slower than GTD cars in corners for a few laps. Incidents cited: a crash on cold tyres at Long Beach Turn 1 at a restart, and a Cadillac pole-sitter losing control on cold tyres during warm-up at Road America. (search summary; page returned 429) — [Racer, Solving GTP's cold tire conundrum](https://racer.com/2023/09/16/solving-gtps-cold-tire-conundrum)
- [PRO] Long Beach 2023: Porsche Penske's #6 963 went the whole 100-minute race on one set and won; the no-tyre stop exploited GTP warm-up problems. **Date conflict:** one search summary placed this in 2024; Racer and the sibling notes put it in 2023 (Porsche 963's first win). — [Racer](https://racer.com/2023/09/16/solving-gtps-cold-tire-conundrum); [Motorsport.com race report](https://www.motorsport.com/imsa/news/imsa-long-beach-porsche-wins-wild-race-taylor-crashes-out/10456884/)
- [PRO] Detroit 2026: one-set is the convention at 100-minute street races, but the #31 Cadillac changed only the right-side tyres at its single stop to be ready for caution restarts; Jack Aitken: "we kind of flipped the script a little bit on that one," and warm-up "was actually really good for just the one-sided tires." ## Car control at the limit: friction circle (G-G), combined braking and cornering, consistency as a sign of control, avoiding mistakes over long stints, and what "smooth" means in data

### Takeaway
The tyre has one grip budget shared between braking/accelerating and cornering; a G-G plot shows how well the driver uses it. Good drivers fill the edge of the envelope with curved transitions from braking into cornering and from cornering into drive; a straight line between peak braking and peak cornering means grip left unused during the transition. "Smooth" in tyre terms means not asking for more slip than the corner needs: precise steering turned only as far as necessary, progressive throttle, and no stabs.

### Cited Findings
- [PRO, data training] A G-G diagram plots lateral vs longitudinal acceleration for every sample, so a lap becomes a cloud. Useful information sits in the transitions between pure braking and pure cornering, and between cornering and acceleration. Novices often show a near-straight line between peak braking and peak cornering, indicating unused grip in the transition. When a tyre is loaded in combined braking and turning, it can't brake as hard as in a straight line. Braking usually exceeds acceleration, so the shape is egg-like rather than circular. (search summary of vendor/educational pages) — [HP Academy, G-G diagram](https://www.hpacademy.com/courses/professional-motorsport-data-analysis/cornering-performance-gg-diagram/); [HP Academy, traction circle](https://www.hpacademy.com/courses/data-analysis-fundamentals/the-data-analysis-tool-box-traction-circle/); [PURPL](https://purpl.app/blog/friction-circle-karting/)
- [PRO] Overlaying brake and throttle state on the G-G cloud shows where a fast driver brakes and where they apply throttle around the circle. (forum discussion, not authoritative) — [Autosport forum](https://forums.autosport.com/topic/67990-friction-circle)
- [PRO] Steering should be precise and smooth, turned only as much as the corner requires; abrupt side-to-side steering is mainly for cleaning tyres and adds heat; drivers running slightly under peak slip angle keep grip in reserve and save tyres. — [NASA Speed News](https://nasaspeed.news/tech/suspension/tire-management-how-to-get-the-most-out-of-your-race-tires-from-behind-the-wheel/)
- [SIM] "Consistent lap times and smooth driving help manage wear and keep temperatures in range." — [Coach Dave Academy](https://coachdaveacademy.com/tutorials/understanding-tire-pressures-in-iracing/)
- [SIM] iRacing GTP after the 2025 update: under/oversteer develop progressively, giving time to react, with clear feedback at the limit. (single reviewer) — [Box This Lap](https://boxthislap.org/iracing-this-time-the-tyres-feel-alive/)
- [SIM] iRacing GT3 2025: slight excess throttle can snap the car, so throttle smoothness matters most. — [Coach Dave Academy](https://coachdaveacademy.com/announcements/the-2025-iracing-gt3-tyre-model-update-explained/)
- [SIM] Skid-pad work (100-ft radius) to learn how braking and throttle change the car's path, credited by Alexander for his pace. (real-world practice tip) — [NASA Speed News](https://nasaspeed.news/tech/suspension/tire-management-how-to-get-the-most-out-of-your-race-tires-from-behind-the-wheel/)

### Inferences
- "Smooth" measured in data, as a working definition for the assistant (inferred, built from the sourced principles above): (1) steering: fewer and smaller corrections, and peak steering angle per corner close to the minimum seen on the driver's good laps at the same speed; (2) brakes: one firm application, then a steady, monotonic release into the corner without re-application; (3) throttle: a progressive ramp from apex to full, no on-off-on, and no wheelspin spikes; (4) G-G: curved, filled transitions between the braking and lateral peaks, not a straight chord or a notch toward the origin.
- Consistency as a control metric: low lap-to-lap variance at a given fuel/tyre state, and low variance of the same corner's minimum speed and brake point across laps. Rising variance late in a stint, with lap times dropping off faster than the fuel-corrected trend, is a likely sign of tyres going off or driver fatigue; the assistant can't separate the two without tyre data. (inferred)
- Because slip is the heat source, a car "sliding a lot but fast" in one lap is spending tyre life; the G-G envelope radius may look good while the steering trace shows extra lock. Checking steering against lateral G (more steering for the same lateral G = understeer/scrub) ties car control to tyre wear. (inferred)
