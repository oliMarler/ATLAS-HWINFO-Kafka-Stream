# Chief engineer and engineering hierarchy in endurance/sportscar racing

Scope note: roughly 15 tool calls. Several fetches failed (Porsche team page 404, Racer 429, Hertz page empty). Most sources are team announcements, not role explainers. Nothing found describes a chief engineer's day-to-day in IMSA/WEC. Role boundaries below are therefore thin and partly inferred. The Vasselon Q&A is from 2012 (LMP1 era), outside the preferred 2020-2026 window.

## 1. What is the chief engineer's responsibility compared with the race engineer and technical director?

### Takeaway
Titles are not standardised. Public sources show the chief engineer, technical director and "lead race engineer" as distinct, programme-level posts above per-car race engineers, but none defines their remits precisely. Treat the split as team-specific.

### Cited Findings
- Genesis Magma Racing (WEC Hypercar, 2026) lists team principal Cyril Abiteboul, team manager Anouck Abadie, chief engineer Justin Taylor, sporting director Gabriele Tarquini, and technical director François-Xavier Demaison as separate posts. Taylor's brief is to develop "our team and the GMR-001 Hypercar from the ground up". Taylor has Le Mans wins with Audi and Ferrari and Cadillac wins at Sebring and Petit Le Mans. The article gives no detail on the technical director's responsibilities — [Racer/The Race, 13 Jun 2025](https://www.the-race.com/endurance/touring-car-legend-gets-big-role-in-hyundais-new-wec-programme.md) (title and details as fetched; Racer URL https://racer.com/2025/06/13/genesis-magma-racing-adds-key-staff returned 429 so was not read directly)
- Porsche Penske Motorsport (PPM) added a "lead race engineer" layer for IMSA in Jan 2024. Brandon Fry (ex BMW RLL technical director; previously Chip Ganassi Racing until end 2017) supports the two car race engineers, adds capacity, and oversees people in the background including performance engineers. PPM also added one or two performance engineers after reviewing its first GTP year. Fry sits at the top of the IMSA race engineering group. Managing director Jonathan Diuguid: "He adds some capacity to cover all the bases" and "to make sure we get the maximum out of the car" — [Motorsport.com, Charles Bradley, 16 Jan 2024](https://www.motorsport.com/imsa/news/porsche-bmw-technical-director-963-imsa-gtp/10566451/)
- A generic guide (F1/NASCAR-oriented, not endurance) says the chief engineer's race-time job is keeping the car reliable and within operating limits and being first contact with the FIA on technical regulation. The technical director covers track and factory technical operations, training, deadlines, sometimes budgets — summarised from [motorsportexplained.com](https://motorsportexplained.com/type-of-f1-engineers-trackside-during-race-weekend) (search snippet only; F1 comparison, low confidence for endurance)
- United Autosports created a Technical Director role to build its engineering department for its WEC debut, covering WEC, European Le Mans and Michelin Le Mans Cup — [United Autosports](https://unitedautosports.com/news/david-greenwood-joins-united-autosports-as-technical-director) (search snippet only)
- Ray Lee is "technical director" at Compass Racing (GT4/GT3 McLaren customer team), showing the title also covers a small customer team's lead engineer — [Sportscar365](https://sportscar365.com/imsa/iwsc/michelin-inside-the-role-engineers/)
- Brown (Juncos Cadillac DPi engineer): "I always believe a good race engineer needs to have raced" and "Find the right group of individuals, give them a bit of direction and stand back and let them work" — [Sportscar365](https://sportscar365.com/imsa/iwsc/michelin-inside-the-role-engineers/)
- Porsche's management line (from a Porsche newsroom PDF, date not shown, probably early 963 years): Urs Kuratle oversees for Porsche Motorsport as director of LMDh Factory Motorsport; Jonathan Diuguid is managing director of PPM; Travis Law is competition director; Christian Eifrig is technical project manager — [Porsche newsroom PDF](https://newsroom.porsche.com/pdf/a4b7a6cb-73e8-49b8-a3b2-290df7c9a145) (search-snippet summary, not read in full)

### Inferences
- A plausible pattern: technical director/head of engineering owns concept, factory/simulation resources, regulation and BoP liaison; chief/lead engineer owns trackside engineering quality across cars; race engineer owns one car's setup and radio. This is inferred from titles and the Fry hire, not stated by any source.
- Note that Fry held a "technical director" title at RLL and a "lead race engineer" title at PPM, so title and seniority do not map one to one.

### Gaps
- No endurance-specific source defining chief engineer remit (concept, setup philosophy, BoP, driver line-up, risk calls). Not found in this pass.
- Who owns driver and engineer line-up decisions: no source found (likely team principal/management; unverified).
- Toyota's current technical structure after Vasselon: a Spanish report (Diario Motor, Jan 2024) says David Floury replaced him as technical director — single source, snippet only, not read: [Diario Motor](https://www.diariomotor.com/competicion/noticia/pascal-vasselon-deja-la-direccion-tecnica-de-toyota-en-el-wec)

## 2. How is decision authority split during a race?

### Takeaway
The best primary statement found (Toyota's Vasselon, 2012) is a three-tier model: immediate calls by the car's race engineer, time-available strategic calls with technical director input, higher-level calls through team management. Current-era (2020-2026) sources on who calls stops, and how disagreements are resolved, were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Pascal Vasselon, Toyota (Autosport Q&A by Stuart Codling, 13 June 2012, LMP1 hybrid era): "If an immediate decision has to be made, that is the responsibility of the car's race engineer." For decisions with more time, e.g. "which tyres to fit for the next stint", he will be involved. "Higher level decisions will go through the team management." Same Q&A: two cars shared staff (car 7 engineer from ORECA, car 8 from Toyota Motorsport, chief mechanics swapped) — "We are one team rather than two." — [Autosport, 2012](https://www.autosport.com/wec/news/100341/q-and-a-with-pascal-vasselon)
- Toyota's Le Mans 2021 fuel-pressure fix was a pit-wall engineering decision: a software workaround was chosen over a garage stop that would have cost at least 45 minutes (per Vasselon) — [Motorsport.com](https://www.motorsport.com/wec/news/toyotas-fuel-issue-could-have-killed-le-mans-victory-hopes/6652119/) (snippet only)
- Toyota team orders: at Spa the running order was frozen before the final stops with drivers agreeing not to race after the last stop — [The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/accelerator/20662/toyota-fixed-running-order-potentially-orchestrated-alonsos-wec-win-at-spa); at Portimao cars raced to the flag under a rule that the faster car moves ahead — [Autosport](https://www.autosport.com/wec/news/toyota-explains-portimao-wec-team-orders-call/6567388/) (both snippet only; year not confirmed)
- Porsche profile: Jean-Philippe Sarrazin, race engineer on the #6 963, "oversees all activity" on the car from the pit wall and manages run plans and oversees mechanics and engineers — [Porsche Motorsport](https://racing.porsche.com/team-members/jean-philippe-sarrazin) (via search snippet; page returned 404 on direct fetch)
- Sarrazin moved to Ford's 2027 WEC Hypercar programme (after engineering PPM's 2024 WEC championship car) alongside Leena Gade (race engineer) and Grant Clarke (Trackside Engineering Manager, ex Formula E) — [Car Racing Reporter](https://www.carracingreporter.com/home/wec-2027-ford-hypercar)
- A Hertz Racing Gold blog says the race engineer works with the team principal and drivers on strategy and is the driver's main radio contact — [Hertz](https://www.hertz.com/us/en/racing-gold/blog/who-makes-up-wec-team) (search snippet only; page content did not load on fetch; consumer-marketing source, low reliability)
- Setup authority between drivers and engineers (Sportscar365 interviews): Braun says setup is built around the driver with most time in the car; Lee says in earlier years he set up for the fastest driver so the amateur adapted; Brown says drivers "have to make it work" — [Sportscar365](https://sportscar365.com/imsa/iwsc/michelin-inside-the-role-engineers/)
- Action Express team manager Gary Nelson (ex NASCAR crew chief): "You have to keep the wheels turning" (2018) — [IMSA](https://www.imsa.com/news/2018/03/01/keep-the-wheels-turning-action-express-racings-secret-sauce/) (snippet only). AXR draws on NASCAR staff for crew roles — [Sportscar365](https://sportscar365.com/imsa/iwsc/axr-tapped-into-nascar-to-staff-no-48-entry/) (snippet only)

### Inferences
- Radio voice: the race engineer is the one driver contact in nearly every source found; strategist and chief engineer likely feed the race engineer rather than talk to the driver. For a sim assistant, a single voice (race engineer) with strategy input relayed is the safest mapping.
- The Vasselon tiers suggest classifying messages by urgency: immediate (race engineer), planned-stint (engineer plus senior input), strategic or sporting (management).
- Crew chief in IMSA/endurance is a mechanic-side role (pit crew, car prep), distinct from the engineer; the AXR NASCAR link supports this but no source states it outright.

### Gaps
- Who formally calls the pit stop (strategist vs race engineer vs team manager) in GTP/Hypercar: not found.
- How disagreements are resolved on the pit wall: not found, except Vasselon's escalation tiers.
- Current-era (2020-2026) statements on final say: not found.

## 3. Team structure by car/programme (engineers per car, shared data engineers, strategists, tyre engineers, sim group)

### Takeaway
Public sources confirm a race engineer per car, performance engineers added as support, a management layer above, and a split between mirrored sites at Porsche. Data engineer, strategist, tyre engineer and simulation roles are only mentioned in passing.

### Cited Findings
- PPM runs parallel structures at Mooresville (IMSA) and Mannheim (WEC), each with a team manager and head mechanic, two vehicle heads (car chiefs), racing engineers and mechanics — [Porsche newsroom PDF](https://newsroom.porsche.com/pdf/a4b7a6cb-73e8-49b8-a3b2-290df7c9a145) (snippet summary)
- PPM's 2024 expansion added performance engineers and a lead race engineer layer — [Motorsport.com](https://www.motorsport.com/imsa/news/porsche-bmw-technical-director-963-imsa-gtp/10566451/)
- Chip Ganassi Racing promoted simulation engineer Danielle Shepherd to race engineer when it expanded to two IMSA Cadillac DPi-V.R cars (Dec 2021), showing a sim-to-race-engineer career path — [Racer](https://racer.com/2021/12/14/ganassi-promotes-shepherd-to-race-engineer-for-imsa-team/) (snippet only)
- Corvette Racing is run as Corvette Racing by Pratt Miller Motorsports (two Z06 GT3.R in GTD Pro since 2024); Pratt Miller did chassis engineering, GM did the engine; named figures Brandon Widmer (VP motorsports), Christie Bagne (program manager), Marc Maurini (program director, 2023 report). No chief engineer named in what was found — [Endurance-Info](https://en.endurance-info.com/auto/article/107676-imsa-wec-gtwc-testing-all-you-need-know-about-corvette-z06-gt3r-programme), [Car & Motorsport reports via search](https://au.motorsport.com/imsa/news/corvette-2024-gtd-pro-confirmed/10486437/) (snippets only)
- Job listings (low-grade evidence): a race engineer role includes analysing telemetry for setup and strategy and partnering with the race strategist on stop plans; a BMW M Team WRT GTP listing says electrical technicians work with engineers and data specialists — [W Racing Team careers](https://www.w-racingteam.com/racing/careers) (snippet only)
- Small-team example: Compass Racing (GT4/GT3 McLaren) has a technical director and car engineers; CORE autosport and Juncos each list one engineer per car in the Sportscar365 piece — [Sportscar365](https://sportscar365.com/imsa/iwsc/michelin-inside-the-role-engineers/)
- F1/IndyCar comparison only: Racer's IndyCar piece likens engineer to head coach and strategist to coordinator, with some cars combining the two roles — [Racer](https://racer.com/2019/03/08/insight-2019-indycar-race-engineers-and-race-strategists/) (snippet only; IndyCar, not endurance)

### Inferences
- Likely shape for a factory GTP/Hypercar car: race engineer, performance engineer(s), data/systems engineer, plus tyre support from the supplier (Michelin in IMSA/WEC) and a strategist shared across the programme. This is not documented in the sources found.

### Gaps
- Real org charts for Toyota, Ferrari AF Corse, Acura/Meyer Shank, BMW/RLL, Cadillac: not found. Ferrari AF Corse and Acura/MSR had no usable hits.
- Tyre engineer roles (team vs Michelin) and simulation/vehicle-dynamics group structure: not found.
- Engineers-per-car counts and shared data engineers: not found.

## 4. What chief engineers look at (and ignore) in driver feedback and data; briefing and meeting rhythm

### Takeaway
No source found covers chief engineer assessment of feedback, briefing format or meeting cadence in endurance racing. Only fragmentary driver-vs-engineer setup philosophy was found.

### Cited Findings
- Setup is built around the driver with most seat time in amateur-heavy GT/DPi crews (Braun, Lee) — [Sportscar365](https://sportscar365.com/imsa/iwsc/michelin-inside-the-role-engineers/)
- Braun: "The questions are always the same, but the answers are always different." Lee: "Testing is key. With tires, it's really about testing the product." — [Sportscar365](https://sportscar365.com/imsa/iwsc/michelin-inside-the-role-engineers/)
- Hybrid-era trade-off (2012 Toyota): drivers reported strong boost, then "there is nothing left!"; the team chose stored energy for performance, target lap time or fuel saving "depends what scenarios we encounter during the race" — [Autosport, 2012](https://www.autosport.com/wec/news/100341/q-and-a-with-pascal-vasselon)

### Inferences
- A sim assistant can reasonably model: trust the driver on feel and balance direction, check magnitude against data, prefer one change at a time. This is general engineering practice, not evidenced by these sources.

### Gaps
- Pre-race briefing, stint review and debrief rhythms: not found.
- What chief engineers deliberately ignore: not found.

## 5. Skills, careers, and the hard parts of the job

### Takeaway
Observed paths: simulation engineer to race engineer (Shepherd), engineering management hires from other series (Clarke from Formula E, Fry from BMW RLL technical director), and chief engineers with long Le Mans records (Taylor). Practitioner statements on difficulty are limited.

### Cited Findings
- Sim engineer to race engineer: Danielle Shepherd at Ganassi — [Racer](https://racer.com/2021/12/14/ganassi-promotes-shepherd-to-race-engineer-for-imsa-team/)
- Braun chose engineering college "to help me figure out how to make race cars go faster"; Brown says too few young race engineers get to design before they "touch the headsets" — [Sportscar365](https://sportscar365.com/imsa/iwsc/michelin-inside-the-role-engineers/)
- Leena Gade: first female race engineer to win Le Mans, three titles with Audi, later Multimatic, now Ford 2027 race engineer — [Car Racing Reporter](https://www.carracingreporter.com/home/wec-2027-ford-hypercar)
- Justin Taylor: Le Mans wins with Audi and Ferrari, Cadillac wins at Sebring and Petit Le Mans — [The Race](https://www.the-race.com/endurance/touring-car-legend-gets-big-role-in-hyundais-new-wec-programme.md)
- United Autosports technical director hire was ex Ferrari F1 race engineer (name in source: David Greenwood) — [United Autosports](https://unitedautosports.com/news/david-greenwood-joins-united-autosports-as-technical-director) (snippet only)

### Inferences
- Cross-series movement and senior-engineer migration suggest the chief engineer pool is small and relationship-driven; evidence is anecdotal.
