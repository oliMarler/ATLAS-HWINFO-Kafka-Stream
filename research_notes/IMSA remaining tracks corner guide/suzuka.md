# Suzuka International Racing Course, Grand Prix layout: corner guide

## What are the official numbers, names and directions of the 18 corners?

### Takeaway
The F1/GP layout has 18 numbered turns. The best single table found is from a secondary newsletter (f1da), and it agrees with Wikipedia and other partial sources on the anchor points (Dunlop = T7, Degner = T8/T9, Hairpin = T11, Spoon = T13/T14, 130R = T15, Casio Triangle = T16/T17). Official suzukacircuit.jp naming and directions could not be fetched. Treat directions for T1, T2, T10, T12 and T18 as moderately verified.

### Cited Findings
Lap order from start/finish (turn, name, direction, character), all from the f1da table unless noted:
- T1 "First Curve": right, fast sweeping, slight lift, runs straight into T2. Name "First Curve" is the common name, not given in f1da. [f1da](https://f1da.beehiiv.com/p/japan-gp-sector-by-sector-breakdown)
- T2 "Second Curve": right, tight, sharp braking while still turning. [f1da](https://f1da.beehiiv.com/p/japan-gp-sector-by-sector-breakdown). Another source treats T1-2 as one "fast double-apex" corner taken in third gear. [search summary of Wikipedia/other](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuka_Circuit)
- T3-T6 "S Curves" (esses): T3 left, T4 right, T5 left, T6 right (T6 slightly slower exit). [f1da](https://f1da.beehiiv.com/p/japan-gp-sector-by-sector-breakdown)
- T7 "Dunlop Curve": left, long sweeping, flat out; track climbs into it. Wikipedia confirms Dunlop is turn 7; run-off doubled 12 m to 25 m in 2002 and corner made slightly tighter. [f1da](https://f1da.beehiiv.com/p/japan-gp-sector-by-sector-breakdown), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuka_International_Racing_Course)
- T8 "Degner 1": right, medium speed, early braking, uses kerb. T9 "Degner 2": right, tight, hard braking. Named for Ernst Degner, who crashed there in 1962; split into two corners in 1987. [f1da](https://f1da.beehiiv.com/p/japan-gp-sector-by-sector-breakdown), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuka_International_Racing_Course)
- After Degner the track passes under the overpass (the figure-8 crossover). [search summary](https://www.crash.net/f1/feature/43764/1/jacques-villeneuves-lap-of-suzuka)
- T10: right, slight kink, full throttle into hairpin braking zone; no common name. Hairpin braking happens during this fast right. [f1da](https://f1da.beehiiv.com/p/japan-gp-sector-by-sector-breakdown)
- T11 "Hairpin": left, tight, slowest corner (1st/2nd gear), roughly 60 km/h per one source; frequent lock-ups. [f1da](https://f1da.beehiiv.com/p/japan-gp-sector-by-sector-breakdown)
- T12: right, long, sets up Spoon (200R region; Wikipedia mentions a chicane between hairpin and 200R only on the motorcycle layout). [f1da](https://f1da.beehiiv.com/p/japan-gp-sector-by-sector-breakdown)
- T13-T14 "Spoon Curve": double-apex left, first apex delicate braking, exit is key for the long back straight (about 1.2 km, passes over the front section on the overpass). [f1da](https://f1da.beehiiv.com/p/japan-gp-sector-by-sector-breakdown), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuka_International_Racing_Course)
- T15 "130R": left, long, flat out over 300 km/h (about 295 km/h entry cited elsewhere). Wikipedia diagram labels it turn 15. Reprofiled as double-apex after McNish's 2002 crash; Kato fatal crash 2003 was on the approach to the chicane. [f1da](https://f1da.beehiiv.com/p/japan-gp-sector-by-sector-breakdown), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuka_International_Racing_Course), [RaceFans](https://www.racefans.net/going-to-a-race/suzuka-circuit-japan/)
- T16-T17 "Casio Triangle" (final chicane): T16 left, heavy braking; T17 right, exit matters for the run to the line. Added 1983 (RaceFans says added for F1's 1987 arrival; sources conflict on date); in 2003 made slightly faster and moved closer to 130R. [f1da](https://f1da.beehiiv.com/p/japan-gp-sector-by-sector-breakdown), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuka_International_Racing_Course); date conflict: [RaceFans](https://www.racefans.net/going-to-a-race/suzuka-circuit-japan/)
- T18: right, small kink/long final right onto the pit straight, full throttle. [f1da](https://f1da.beehiiv.com/p/japan-gp-sector-by-sector-breakdown)
- Lap length 5.807 km, 18 turns, figure-eight, 1.2 km back straight crosses over the front section. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuka_International_Racing_Course), [RaceFans](https://www.racefans.net/going-to-a-race/suzuka-circuit-japan/)
- Grid to Turn 1: 405 m. Longest flat-out section 994 m, max 328 km/h, 66% full throttle (F1 data). [RaceFans](https://www.racefans.net/going-to-a-race/suzuka-circuit-japan/)
- Sectors (F1-style): S1 = T1-T7 (to Dunlop), S2 = T8-T14 (Degners to Spoon), S3 = T15-T18 (130R to line). [f1da](https://f1da.beehiiv.com/p/japan-gp-sector-by-sector-breakdown). Exact timing-line positions in metres not found.

### Inferences
- Sequence by direction: R R | L R L R | L | R R | R | L | R | L L | L | L R | R. Left-handers: T3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 14, 15, 16. Right-handers: T1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 17, 18.
- For an app: group T3-6 as "S Curves", T8-9 as "Degners", T13-14 as "Spoon", T16-17 as "Casio Triangle".

### Gaps
- No distance-from-start/finish figures for any corner found.
- Official Suzuka Circuit course-guide naming (e.g. whether T10 or T12 carry names such as "200R" in the official guide) not retrieved; suzukacircuit.jp returned no course content and Wikipedia text read did not list all turns.
- T1/T2 direction rests on one secondary source (f1da); T18 described inconsistently (some sources fold it into the Casio sequence).
- Older layout (pre-2003) detail beyond the chicane/130R changes not researched.

## Pit lane position, length and speed limit

### Takeaway
Real-world pit lane is on the start/finish straight; F1 limit is 80 km/h. No source gave pit entry/exit corner relative position, pit lane length, or the IMSA/iRacing limit.

### Cited Findings
- F1 pit lane speed limit 80 km/h in practice, qualifying and race. [f1technical](https://www.f1technical.net/news/28393) (page fetch blocked; figure from search snippet), [Formula 1](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/explained-why-are-there-pit-lane-speed-limits-in-f1-and-how-are-they-measured.jc4jiWVsfbRhb62QomSf0.jc4jiWVsfbRhb62QomSf0)
- A Suzuka 1000km paid test regulation document cites 50 km/h in the pit lane, conflicting with 80 km/h (series-specific). [IGTC doc](https://www.intercontinentalgtchallenge.com/documents/notice/3737/Suzuka+1000KM+Paid+Test+Sessions+Regulations.pdf)
- F1 pit lane time loss 17.65 s. [RaceFans](https://www.racefans.net/going-to-a-race/suzuka-circuit-japan/)

### Inferences
- Pit entry is presumably after the Casio Triangle/T18 and exit after T1 area, but this is not source-confirmed.

### Gaps
- Pit entry/exit location, pit lane length, IMSA/iRacing limit: not found. Check iRacing in-sim track info or IMSA sporting regs.

## Overtaking places and hard-to-pass sections

### Takeaway
Few documented spots; sources point to the pit straight into T1, the T11 hairpin and the Casio Triangle (T16) as the main passes. Nothing multi-class specific found.

### Cited Findings
- Hairpin T11: possible but needs perfect timing; Casio Triangle T16: the final overtaking spot; T12 explicitly not an overtaking spot. [f1da](https://f1da.beehiiv.com/p/japan-gp-sector-by-sector-breakdown)
- F1 DRS zone: one, on the pit straight. [RaceFans](https://www.racefans.net/going-to-a-race/suzuka-circuit-japan/)

### Inferences
- Esses (T3-T7) and Degners are effectively no-pass for traffic; faster class likely needs the 130R exit/Casio, pit straight, or hairpin. Unverified for IMSA.

### Gaps
- No multi-class/endurance-specific overtaking guidance found.

## Elevation, figure-8 bridge, surface

### Takeaway
Figure-8 with the back straight (after Spoon, towards 130R) crossing over the section after Degner 2.

### Cited Findings
- Back straight passes over the front section on an overpass; only FIA Grade 1 figure-8. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuka_International_Racing_Course)
- Large part of track resurfaced, from last chicane exit to end of Sector 1 (as of the f1da article, F1 year not stated). [f1da](https://f1da.beehiiv.com/p/japan-gp-sector-by-sector-breakdown)
- Track climbs into Dunlop. [search summary](https://www.crash.net/f1/feature/43764/1/jacques-villeneuves-lap-of-suzuka)

### Gaps
- No elevation figures, no banking figures verified (Degner 2 described as banked by Villeneuve only).

## iRacing version

### Takeaway
iRacing offers Grand Prix, Moto, East, West, West Chicane layouts. Whether numbering matches the real 18 turns is not verified.

### Cited Findings
- iRacing GP course 5.808 km; Moto 5.821 km; East 2.243 km; West 3.467 km. [simracing.wiki](https://www.simracing.wiki/IRacing_Suzuka_International_Racing_Course)
- A 2011 iRacing announcement called the GP "17 turn", versus 18 in the real count; cause unconfirmed (iRacing may count the Casio chicane differently). [search summary](https://www.iracing.com/iracing-com-japan-gp-slated-for-october-8/)

### Inferences
- Do not assume iRacing numbering equals 1-18; verify in-sim, possibly via a runtime turn list.

### Gaps
- iRacing corner numbering, pit lane limit and layout differences: not found.
