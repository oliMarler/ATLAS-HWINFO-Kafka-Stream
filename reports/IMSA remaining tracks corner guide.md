# Four tracks mapped corner by corner, gaps flagged

**Only one of the four tracks has a corner list that can be trusted without caveats, and that one comes from your telemetry (Long Beach).** The research notes support complete corner-by-corner tables for Sebring (17 turns, names well corroborated, directions from one source), Suzuka Grand Prix (18 turns, all from one secondary table) and Le Mans (named sections with directions, but no official numbering). Long Beach has almost no sourced directions for T1-T8, but nothing found contradicts your telemetry (T1 left, T2-T5 right, T6-T7 left, T8-T9 right, T10 left, T11 right hairpin). Three corrections from the research: the Le Mans Mulsanne chicanes date from **1990**, not 2018; Sebring's **Sunset Bend is Turn 17** (Turn 10 is Cunningham Corner); Long Beach is **3.167 km (1.968 mi)** with 11 turns. Pit speed limits are known only for Le Mans (60 km/h, 2026 WEC briefing) and Sebring (60 km/h, from a Mustang Challenge briefing). Nobody found iRacing's in-sim corner numbering for any of the four, so for the race-engineer app the corner tables below are a starting point to check against a telemetry lap, not a final source of truth. Each track ends with a Gaps section, and the cross-track Gaps summary comes last.

**Confidence key used in all tables:** **C** = confirmed by two or more independent sources; **S** = single source; **I** = inferred from other findings (no source says it directly). Anything read only from a search snippet is marked **S (snippet)** and should be treated as weak.

## Long Beach: eleven turns, 3.167 km, directions from your telemetry

Long Beach is a 1.968 mi (**3.167 km**), 11-turn street circuit with tight 90-degree corners and concrete walls ([RaceSchool](https://www.raceschool.com/tracks/long-beach-street-circuit/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_of_Long_Beach)). It has had the same length since 2000, when the current 11-turn layout began; 1992-98 had 9 turns and 1999 had 13 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach_Street_Circuit); [RacingCircuits.info](https://www.racingcircuits.info/north-america/usa/long-beach.html)). The hairpin is **Turn 11** on this layout, the slowest corner of the season at about 30 mph ([Fox Sports](https://www.foxsports.com/stories/motor/indycar-analysis-james-hinchcliffe-long-beach)). No source gave official corner names beyond "hairpin" and the "dolphin fountain section" (T2-T3), and no source gave distances from start/finish. The only sourced directions are T9 right, T10 left and T11 right (the first from a snippet only). Directions below therefore come from your telemetry; the "Sourced description" column shows what the notes add.

Pits and paddock sit on Shoreline Drive beside the traditional start line (since 1983), and cars leave T11 "past the pit entrance" ([RacingCircuits.info](https://www.racingcircuits.info/north-america/usa/long-beach.html); [NBC Los Angeles](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/sports/grand-prix-long-beach-street-course-map/3673379/)). It follows (**I**) that start/finish and pit lane share the straight between T11 and T1, with pit exit near T1 (a Le Mans Ultimate guide snippet says the same; **S, snippet**). The pit speed limit, pit lane length and sector lines were **not found**. T1 is the main passing place, set up by the run out of the hairpin; T9 is second best; a late-race traffic jam at the hairpin changed the previous year's IMSA race ([NBC Los Angeles](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/sports/grand-prix-long-beach-street-course-map/3673379/)). The T2-T8 section is probably hard to pass (**I**: narrow, walled, tight). Elevation: fall at T4, rise into T5, fall into T6 with a mid-corner climb, fall into T8 ([Fox Sports](https://www.foxsports.com/stories/motor/indycar-analysis-james-hinchcliffe-long-beach)); surface and kerb data were not found. iRacing offers one configuration, "Grand Prix Circuit", 1.968 mi and 11 turns ([SimRacingWiki](https://simracing.wiki/Long_Beach_Street_Circuit_(iRacing))), which matches the real count.

### Long Beach gaps and conflicts

The main gap is T1-T8 directions: no source stated them, so your telemetry is the only authority there. One unresolved conflict: NBC describes "a sharp right turn" onto Seaside Way after a short straight past The Pike but does not number it. If that corner is T7, it conflicts with your T7 left; if it is T8, it fits. Resolve it against the track map. NBC's "clockwise" description is unreliable and should be ignored. Also missing: official corner names (Pine Avenue and Aquarium Way were not mapped to turns), corner distances, pit limit and length, sector lines, class-specific passing advice (GTP/GTD), surface and kerb detail. Several sources (imsa.com, racer.com, overtake.gg) returned 403 or 429 and were seen only as search snippets. The iRacing numbering is unverified, so confirm it with a lap of telemetry.

## Sebring: seventeen turns, Sunset Bend is T17, Hairpin direction disputed

The current Sebring layout dates from 1999, is **3.740 mi (6.019 km)** with 17 turns (the same Wikipedia page's body text says 3.741 mi / 6.021 km) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebring_International_Raceway)). The names are corroborated by an Le Mans-organiser history ([24h-lemans.com](https://24h-lemans.com/en/news/12-hours-of-sebring-legendary-race-track-6374)), a track clinic ([Yahoo Autos](https://autos.yahoo.com/track-clinic-everything-know-master-152100390.html)) and a sim guide ([Racecontrol](https://news.racecontrol.gg/?p=865)). Turn 3 is Kristensen Corner ([Sportscar365](https://sportscar365.com/imsa/sebrings-turn-3-officially-named-kristensen-corner/)). The directions column rests on the Racecontrol guide alone (a Le Mans Ultimate guide whose numbering matches the real track), so it is **S** throughout unless another source is shown. The correction that matters: **Sunset Bend is Turn 17** and Cunningham Corner is Turn 10 ([24h-lemans.com](https://24h-lemans.com/en/news/12-hours-of-sebring-legendary-race-track-6374)). "Cunningham Straight" was found nowhere; do not use it. The long straight after T16 is the Ulmann Straight.

Exiting cars stay two wheels right of the yellow line and merge into Turn 1; the restart acceleration zone is T17, which has a local yellow light (same briefing). Pit entry is after T17 (**I**). My inference (**I**) is that the braking zones at T1, T3, T7, T10 and T17 offer passes, and the flat middle (T8-T9, T11-T12, T14-T15) is hard. ### Sebring gaps and conflicts

The **T7 Hairpin direction is disputed** (Racecontrol right, Yahoo implies left); verify on a track map or telemetry before the app calls it. Names for T1, T2, T4, T12 are absent, and T9 has only the shared Fangio name. Whether T6 is Gurney Bend rests on one history, and a second history says Big Bend was renamed Gurney Bend in 1996 (unverified). The Wikipedia length differs by 2 m between its table and body. Kristensen's naming article is dated 2016-03-18 in the fetch while a search summary said 2026; the fetch looks right. The Yahoo article is from 2017 and the track has since been revised and resurfaced, so the 0.9 mi of original concrete figure is probably out of date. Not found: corner distances, pit lane length, IMSA-specific pit limit, sector lines, a driver-sourced IMSA overtaking guide (the McNish article returned 403), iRacing's own labels and layout name (TrackTitan returned 403).

## Suzuka Grand Prix: eighteen turns from one table, anchors cross-checked

The Grand Prix layout is **5.807 km** with 18 turns, a figure-eight whose roughly 1.2 km back straight crosses over the front section ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuka_International_Racing_Course); [RaceFans](https://www.racefans.net/going-to-a-race/suzuka-circuit-japan/)). The turn-by-turn table comes from a single secondary newsletter ([f1da](https://f1da.beehiiv.com/p/japan-gp-sector-by-sector-breakdown)), but its anchor points agree with Wikipedia and other partial sources: Dunlop is T7, Degners are T8-T9, Hairpin is T11, Spoon is T13-T14, 130R is T15, Casio Triangle is T16-T17. ### Suzuka gaps and conflicts

**iRacing may not use 1-18.** A 2011 iRacing announcement called the Grand Prix layout "17 turn" ([iRacing](https://www.iracing.com/iracing-com-japan-gp-slated-for-october-8/), search summary only), and the cause is unconfirmed; the Casio chicane may be counted as one corner, which would shift T16 onward. Check this before the app uses T15-T18. All 18 directions rest on one secondary table, with T1, T2, T10, T12 and T18 the weakest. The Casio chicane date conflicts (Wikipedia: 1983; RaceFans: for F1's 1987 arrival). Also missing: corner distances, official naming, pit lane position and length, an endurance or multi-class passing source, elevation and banking figures (only Villeneuve describes Degner 2 as banked), and iRacing's pit limit.

## Le Mans: named sections and directions, but no official numbers

Le Mans is the weakest fit for a per-number table. The current layout, in use since 2018, is **13.626 km** with 38 turns per Wikipedia, and the race lap record is 3:17.297 (Conway, 2019) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_la_Sarthe)). The corner numbers in the notes come from a Le Mans Ultimate game guide with 33 numbered turns, which is neither official nor iRacing, and the guide admits some directions are inferred ([RaceControl](https://news.racecontrol.gg/tracks/le-mans-ultimate-circuit-de-la-sarthe-le-mans-track-guide/)). The 33-versus-38 difference was not reconciled. **The Mulsanne chicanes date from 1990** (FIA would not sanction a straight over 2 km), not 2018; iRacing's own page also says two chicanes were added in 1990 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_la_Sarthe); [iRacing](https://www.iracing.com/tracks/circuit-de-la-sarthe/)). The 2018 change was to the Tertre Rouge re-profile (summariser wording, so verify). Dunlop Chicane dates from 1987, tightened in 1994 and 2006; the Ford chicanes date from the late 1960s and early 1970s ([ACO](https://24h-lemans.com/en/news/24-hours-of-le-mans-the-legendary-spots-on-the-circuit-de-la-sarthe-19372)). Because there is no official numbering, the app should call corners by name. Game-guide numbers (LMU) are shown only so you can match telemetry.

Pit entry is at the Ford chicane and the pit exit white line is the turn-in reference for Dunlop Curve, so the pit lane spans start/finish (**I**). Ignore the 450 m, 60 km/h alternate pit lane from the 2006 test day; it is not the current one. ### Le Mans gaps and conflicts

**IMSA schedule:** the notes could not confirm that Le Mans is on the IMSA schedule (the brief says it is, the sources do not). Treat the event context as unverified; the track data stands regardless. No official ACO corner numbers, no corner distances, no current pit lane length or entry/exit positions, no sector line positions (WEC uses three sectors plus mini-sectors), and no elevation numbers were found. The reconciliation of 38 turns against the 33 game numbers is open. Unconfirmed directions: second Mulsanne chicane exit, Karting Esses, Arnage. iRacing in-sim labels, whether its chicanes differ from reality, and what the Historic config removes were not found. The 2018 Tertre Rouge detail came through a summarising fetch tool.

## What to do before the app goes live

The research gives a clear order of trust. Names and anchor numbers are solid at Sebring and Suzuka; directions everywhere except Long Beach are one-source and need a telemetry or track-map pass. The cheapest check is one lap per track in iRacing logging steering angle and lap distance: that settles the disputed Sebring T7 Hairpin, the Long Beach T7/T8 "sharp right" question, whether iRacing's Suzuka Grand Prix has 17 or 18 numbered turns, and gives the corner distances no source supplied. Pit limits are the second gap: only the 60 km/h figures at Le Mans and Sebring exist, and both come from briefing documents for other series or events, so read the in-sim pit limit values from iRacing for all four.

## Conclusion

The central finding is that published corner data splits into two kinds: names and anchor numbers, which several sources agree on, and left/right directions, which almost always trace to one guide, often written for a different sim. That makes Long Beach, where you already hold telemetry directions that nothing contradicts, the model for the other three: use the sources for names and character, and let a recorded lap decide direction and distance. Le Mans needs a different approach altogether, calling by name, because no official numbering exists and the one numbered list (33 turns) is a game's. Suzuka's possible 17-versus-18 numbering split in iRacing is the one issue that could silently mislabel half a lap, so check it first.
