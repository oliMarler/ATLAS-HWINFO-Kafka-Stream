# Mapping thin endurance evidence onto a sim engineer

Published practitioner material supports a sim race-engineer and coaching assistant on **how to talk** (short, positive, timed to straights, one fact plus one action), **how to frame energy saving** (lift and coast, which GTP drivers equate with fuel saving), and **how strategies are named and compared** (even, long-short-long, extra stop, last-stop gamble). It does not supply the numbers a developer would most like to hard-code: **no per-circuit pit loss, no seconds per litre, no break-even maths for skipping tyres, no source on who calls the stop, no real radio transcripts, and no symptom-to-setup table from a professional source** were found. Most pages could not be read in full (HTTP 403/429/402, blocked PDFs), so a large share of claims below rest on search-result summaries and are flagged that way. Much of the best-quoted material is from F1 or LMP1, not current IMSA GTP, and the most concrete setup guidance comes from iRacing and Le Mans Ultimate coaching sites. The assistant should therefore be built around quantities it measures itself (pit loss, fuel per lap, tyre fall-off, lap-time variance) and treat everything here as design input rather than as fact tables. Throughout, "Suggestion" and "Inference" paragraphs are mine, not the sources'; "Findings" paragraphs are what a cited page says. Verbatim quotes are omitted because the notes do not mark any as verified in full; sources are paraphrased.

## How to read the evidence: three tiers of confidence

Three tiers recur. The firmest are pages the researchers fetched and read, chiefly the Motorsport.com virtual-energy explainer, the Motorsport.com GTP tyre piece with Connor De Phillippi, the Autosport radio feature (Mercedes' Shovlin, Lotus' Permane), the Porsche LMP1 radio newsroom page, the Sportscar365 "Michelin Insider" pit-stop maths piece, the Sportscar365 engineers piece, and the Autosport 2012 Vasselon interview. The middle tier is search-summary only, including the IMSA and FIA documents, the Racer GTP strategy piece, and most Le Mans 2025 and Petit Le Mans race analyses. The weakest tier is single-source, forum, marketing or content-farm material, and one source the notes explicitly discard (a content-farm page on rear tyre wear and brake bias). Every figure below carries its tier. Two cautions apply across the board: the pro numbers describe real-world IMSA/WEC, not iRacing, whose fuel, tyre and energy rules must be checked separately; and sim-coach guides describe iRacing or Le Mans Ultimate (LMU), which are different products from each other.

## Race engineering: a radio voice with discipline, not a data dump

**Findings.** Practitioners describe the role as technical knowledge, planning and communication, with the endurance version adding duration and real-time fuel, tyre and strategy decisions ([Ford, Leena Gade feature, 2026; promotional in tone](https://www.fromtheroad.ford.com/us/en/articles/2026/leena-gade-ford-racing-wec)). The engineer is the driver's translator to the mechanics, so requests become changes at stops ([FlowRacers, F1 example, search summary](https://flowracers.com/blog/what-do-f1-race-engineers-do)). In IMSA, Porsche Penske layered a lead race engineer (Brandon Fry, ex-BMW RLL) over per-car race engineers in 2024 ([Sportscar365](https://sportscar365.com/imsa/iwsc/fry-joins-porsche-penske-in-team-engineering-expansion/); snippet only). In LMP2, tyre temperature and pressure plus driver feedback are the main stint-performance inputs ([PMW Magazine](https://pmw-magazine.com/news/team-news/goodyear-reveals-how-driver-tire-engineer-relationship-enhances-performance.html)).

The radio guidance is the best-sourced content in the whole body of notes. In a Porsche 919 test, the engineer's calls were short, with a repeated keyword and a driver readback; instructions state the action wanted ("stay out") rather than the one to avoid ("no pit stop"), because a half-heard negative could be misread as an erroneous pit entry; and "copy" is preferred to "yes" ([Porsche Newsroom, LMP1](http://newsroom.porsche.com/en/motorsports/tests-in-monza-10499.html)). Porsche's team leader described the goal as packing maximum information into few words while keeping information flowing when things get hectic (same page). From F1, Mercedes' Shovlin and Lotus' Permane say: keep calls out of braking zones, since corner talk can cost a braking point; give qualifying information before the final sector then stay quiet; deliver information when the driver can act on it; avoid stating the obvious; match tone to the driver, because Räikkönen wanted little and Villeneuve wanted every lap; hold non-urgent items for the pit lane; and stay calm, since a panicking engineer worsens the driver's state ([Autosport radio feature](https://www.autosport.com/f1/news/the-secrets-of-in-race-radio-chat-5102490/5102490/); fetched, summarised by the tool). Silence is also a risk: drivers cannot be left without input on key items (same feature, snippet). Permane also gave tyre-window targets so the driver knows when to conserve or push (same feature).

On live data triggers, Shovlin said engineers must tell drivers about energy-recovery charge and fuel management because they change car balance (F1, same feature, snippet). Debrief practice found is generic: overlay against a reference lap by distance, then drill into throttle, brake and line at the divergence ([GoSetups support doc](https://support.gosetups.gg/hc/en-gb/articles/34406195292690-Understanding-Your-Telemetry-Data)); an F1 fan glossary adds that good debriefs name the reference lap, tyre and fuel load ([F1 Fansite](https://www.f1-fansite.com/glossary/debrief/); low authority).

**Single-source virtual-energy figures; do not use as facts.** One secondary summary states an IMSA GTP 500 kW output cap and a 255.6 kWh stint energy allowance subject to BoP; the notes could not attribute it to either linked page ([Traxion opinion](https://traxion.gg/opinion-why-iracings-hybrid-system-still-needs-some-work/), [RaceControl](https://racecontrol.gg/?p=762)). Likewise, an LMU guide says the NRG gauge reaching 0% gives a 100 s penalty (search snippet; LMU, not iRacing).

**Suggestions (mine, not sourced).** Treat the assistant as one voice with a hard length cap. A template consistent with the sources is attention word, one fact, one action or deadline, expect readback. Phrase positively. Queue non-urgent items and release them on straights or after the stop, suppress them in braking zones, traffic and incidents, and expose a verbosity setting modelled on the Räikkönen-to-Villeneuve spectrum. Normalise fuel and tyre state before comparing laps. Candidate call triggers (energy or fuel delta versus target, tyre out of window, stint-time or box window, class traffic approaching, gap changes) are my synthesis; no source lists endurance trigger thresholds. The fatigue handling suggested by the sources is only a calm, steady tone; added check-ins are my idea. Wrong data produces wrong calls, which this repository's own silent-decoding lesson illustrates; that is internal experience, not research.

**Gaps.** No current-era IMSA/WEC interview on practice and qualifying programmes; no real IMSA/WEC/Le Mans radio transcripts from reliable text sources (video compilations were not accessible); no endurance engineer post-mortems; the Racecar Engineering archive was unreachable. A 1990s Peugeot Le Mans engineer account (calm driver report, short actionable reply) is foundational only ([Autosport](https://www.autosport.com/le-mans/news/how-an-engineer-survives-le-mans/10485835/)).

## Pit strategy: rules and anecdotes are sourced, the costs are not

**Findings: the elements and rules.** The pit lane limit is **60 km/h** in the IMSA consumer guide and in WEC material ([IMSA Understanding the Sport](https://www.imsa.com/weathertech/discover/understanding-the-sport/), [Genesis](https://newsroom.genesis.com/genesis-magma-racings-2026-wec-season-guide-part-3-pit-stops/); both search summary only). The IMSA guide calls a four-tyre change in under **25 s** "typical", a description rather than a rule. A sim coach quotes a prototype tyre-change budget of about **30 s** ([Coach Dave Academy](https://coachdaveacademy.com/tutorials/guide-to-strategy-in-iracing/); extracted by a fetch tool, to be checked against the iRacing car manual). Genesis says its Hypercar takes **80 L in about 40 s** (single source, search summary), which I compute as about 2 L/s, an inference from one team's figure. A Le Mans explainer says refuelling and tyre changing are not done simultaneously, that a fuel-plus-tyre stop takes roughly 20 s with a fresh set adding about 15 s, and that driver swaps are done at tyre stops because the longer stop hides them (approximate journalism) ([24h-lemans.com](https://www.24h-lemans.com/en/news/pit-stop-tactics-a-major-factor-at-the-24-hours-of-le-mans-60740)). A 2018 IMSA bulletin set minimum fill-from-empty times of 30 s (prototypes), 34 s (GTLM) and 40 s (GTD), with no minimum stop time, so short fills were legal; that is pre-GTP and the current values were not confirmed ([Frontstretch](https://frontstretch.com/2018/03/01/imsa-mandates-refueling-times-land-motorsport-penalty)). In 2025 a BMW drew a drive-through for refuelling while on its jacks ([Racer](https://racer.com/2025/01/25/rolex-24-hour-2-pit-error-costs-bmw)).

**Findings: GTP energy and refill.** The virtual tank combines fuel and hybrid torque, with stint energy and refill rate set by BoP; the cited example is **920 MJ** for Cadillac and BMW at the start of a season falling to **902 MJ** by Indianapolis; a first violation costs a **100 s hold** plus the stop, with another 100 s per further offence; the tank only depletes once racing starts; teams need not fill fully (one team figure of about 25 s of fill, roughly two-thirds of a tank); and pit-lane driving is exempt ([Motorsport.com](https://www.motorsport.com/imsa/news/how-virtual-energy-has-become-a-defining-principle-in-hypercar-racing/10531672/); fetched, single article). The 2023 Petit Le Mans stint energies of 915 (Acura), 907 (Porsche) and 903 MJ (Cadillac, BMW) come from a search summary of an IMSA page that was blocked ([IMSA](https://www.imsa.com/news/2023/09/27/high-tech-pit-stops-how-does-gtp-virtual-energy-replenishment-work/)). WEC's 2025 Hypercar decision reportedly sets the refill so full stint energy takes **40 s**, with a BoP docking time, and repays a negative tank at the next stop (search summary; the PDF was unreadable) ([FIA WEC 2025 D06](https://api.fia.com/sites/default/files/wec_2025_d06_hypercar_technical_information_12022025.pdf)). The 40 s rule is WEC-specific; the notes could not confirm IMSA uses it. These MJ and second figures are real-world, not iRacing, and each rests on a single article or summary.

**Findings: when to stop.** The one 2h40 worked example is a VIR sprint-endurance from the GTLM/GTD era: about **60 min** on a full tank, so 160/3 gives a window of about **53 to 54 min**; GTD needed 45 minutes minimum driver time. Named patterns are even stints, Long-Long-Short, Long-Short-Long (a short middle stint gains track position under a yellow), "Ohio", and "Plus One" (an extra stop that risks a lap but gives fresher tyres). Teams run a "ghost car" on pit-wall screens to predict the rejoin position, using in-lap and out-lap times, pit-lane length, box location and service time ([Sportscar365 Michelin Insider](https://sportscar365.com/imsa/iwsc/michelin-imsa-insider-pit-stop-math/); single source, fetched). Last-stop choice is to pit when the window opens hoping a caution reshuffles, or wait for a shorter fill and fresher tyres. Gambles on cautions often fail when the race stays green: a Road America single-stop with a final stint of just over 92 minutes worked after a late yellow ([Motorsport.com](https://www.motorsport.com/imsa/news/road-america-imsa-braun-bennett-win-after-brave-fuel-gamble/3154559/); summary), while a Lime Rock Ford GT fuel gamble lost ([Motorsport.com](https://www.motorsport.com/imsa/news/ford-gt-lime-rock-strategy-933832/); snippet).

**Findings: tyres versus fuel only.** The notes' only evidence is anecdotal. At Long Beach 2023 (100 minutes) Porsche #6 skipped tyres and double-stinted, because of a pace deficit and GTP warm-up difficulty, gaining track position with a much faster stop; the team said the extra set and softer compound in 2024 made it less likely and traffic a bigger concern ([Motorsport.com](https://www.motorsport.com/imsa/news/porsche-963-inspired-long-beach-no-tires-call/10600176/), [Sportscar365](https://sportscar365.com/?p=114708)). Sebring 2026 reportedly turned on a fuel-only call versus a tyre call (search summary) ([Porsche](https://racing.porsche.com/articles/imsa-sebring-race-report-2026)). GTP teams typically double-stint tyres, and a strategist said stints grew from about 35 min in DPi to about 50 min in GTP ([Racer, Jan 2023](https://racer.com/2023/01/28/insight-alternate-strategies-and-new-thinking-for-gtp-race-plans/); snippet). The 35 L tyre threshold in the user's brief is the user's own rule; no source supports or contradicts it.

**Findings: caution handling.** The IMSA full-course-yellow sequence closes pits during pack-up and pass-around, then opens them by class (prototypes first in the Sebring 2026 briefing), with a separate Pit Exit Closed signal ([IMSA Sebring 2026 driver briefing](https://imsa.results.alkamelcloud.com/Results_NoticeBoard/26-2026/06_Sebring%20International%20Raceway/09_Briefing%20Photos/01_IWSC%20DRIVER%20BRIEFING.pdf); search summary). A Ford GT was penalised at Daytona 2019 for entering a closed pit under a red flag ([Autosport](https://www.autosport.com/imsa/news/ford-daytona-loss-after-comeback-from-five-laps-down-a-kick-in-teeth-5283250/5283250/)). The Motorsport.com virtual-energy article implies pace laps under yellow burn less energy than fuel, loosening the energy limit after long cautions.

**Suggestions (inference, unsourced).** Measure pit loss per track from the driver's own data: in-lap plus stationary time plus out-lap, minus two flying laps. Never ship a pit-loss table. Treat fuel-per-second as an iRacing measurement made in practice. Break-even for skipping tyres is inferred as: fuel-only wins when tyre-change time plus new-tyre warm-up loss is less than old-tyre lap-time penalty times laps remaining, plus the value of track position; every input must come from the driver's data, and none was found in sources. Use the sourced pattern names to generate scenario sets. Re-plan after each caution and stop (no source states a cadence). Under yellow, a candidate rule is to stop when inside the fuel window and the pit lane opens before the queue forms, and to stay out after a recent stop; this is unsourced. Rough stop counts at 6/12/24 h (7 to 8, 14 to 16, 28 to 30) from a 50-minute GTP stint ignore cautions and are inference only.

**Gaps.** Per-circuit pit loss; seconds per litre for IMSA classes; current IMSA minimum fill and work-area rules (Articles 32 and 36 of the sporting regulations were not read; the PDF returned 403); undercut and overcut sizes; class pit sequencing; tyre warm-up loss; seconds saved by skipping tyres; IMSA 2026 energy refill rule; red-flag refuelling rules.

## Race strategy: planning from the longest stint, deciding under cautions

**Findings.** Plans start from the longest legal stint and split race time into stints, testing variants and driver minimums. The pit-wall variables listed are weather, fuel economy, traffic, track position, tyre wear and driver rotation, with the ghost-car projection ([Sportscar365 Michelin Insider](https://sportscar365.com/imsa/iwsc/michelin-imsa-insider-pit-stop-math/)). The same piece says IMSA teams are creative, opportunistic and aggressive, and reports green runs as long as 5 h 34 min, so plans banking on cautions often fail.

Cautions reshuffle order by pit timing. Examples from summaries: at Le Mans 2025 cars that had not pitted for slicks before pit closure after a long wet safety car were stuck ([24h-lemans.com](https://www.24h-lemans.com/en/news/24-hours-of-le-mans-philippe-sinault-alpine-its-anybodys-game-56415)); Petit Le Mans 2025 had many cautions and late splash-and-dash efforts ([The Race](https://www.the-race.com/endurance/winners-and-losers-from-imsas-fraught-2025-finale/)); and Porsche Penske reportedly pitted its second car and left the leader out at the 2025 Rolex 24 to deny rivals a wave-around, a single-source, unverified detail ([Speedsport](https://speedsport.com/sports-cars/imsa/three-takeaways-63rd-rolex-24-at-daytona/)). The Rolex 2025 winner was reported inconsistently by a low-quality page, so no result is relied on. For simulation, a 2022 paper uses Monte Carlo rival-position simulation, genetic-algorithm candidate energy strategies and stochastic dynamic programming for hybrid race cars ([arXiv 2203.00084](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.00084); snippet). Multi-class traffic has no authoritative real-world strategy source; only sim-league etiquette rule sets (faster car initiates clean pass, slower car holds line) were found ([TheSimGrid example](https://www.thesimgrid.com/championships/14534/rules); hobbyist).

**Suggestions (inference).** For 2h40, a baseline of three to four stints of roughly 40 to 50 minutes is a starting hypothesis, bounded by iRacing's actual tank and energy rules, which the user must supply. Offer scenario A/B/C from the named patterns. At a caution compare fuel window, rival pit status in class and pit-closed risk. Candidate live KPIs: in-class virtual order after everyone stops, fuel or energy delta versus plan in laps, tyre degradation trend, rejoin traffic, and time to the next caution window; the sourced list is only the pit-wall inputs above. A 2h40 race likely rewards track position and incident avoidance over car preservation; that is not sourced. Add caution probability and rival distributions to any Monte Carlo, per the paper.

**Gaps.** No source on tyre-degradation modelling, energy-per-lap targets, re-plan cadence, car-preservation versus championship risk, quantified time lost in traffic, yielding rules, famous Le Mans calls, or red-flag strategy.

## Driving techniques: lift-and-coast is sourced, but numbers are sim numbers

**Findings.** GTP drivers equate saving energy with saving fuel, mainly through lifting and coasting, and engineers find the energy balance a considerable challenge ([Motorsport.com](https://www.motorsport.com/imsa/news/how-virtual-energy-has-become-a-defining-principle-in-hypercar-racing/10531672/)). A BMW driver in 2023 said teams double-stint some tyres and drivers try to avoid sitting on old ones for long; GTP tyres take much longer to come up to speed, with up to **11 to 13 laps** to switch on in one Long Beach qualifying case (from the fetched page); on old tyres the car becomes a handful; and recent driveability gains came from traction control, braking and hybrid-system behaviour ([Motorsport.com](https://www.motorsport.com/imsa/news/gtp-tire-challenges-allow-drivers-to-make-a-difference/10467756/)). Acura's traction control is in-house and continuously learns grip ([The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/jenson-button-says-acura-imsa-gtp-is-more-complicated-than-an-f1-car-but-why)).

The only hard saving is from LMU, not iRacing: Spa, full fuel, flat out 2:22.5 on 3.45 L versus lift and coast 2:23.2 on 2.0 L, so about **1.45 L for 0.7 s per lap** ([Coach Dave Academy, LMU](https://coachdaveacademy.com/tutorials/mastering-lift-and-coast-how-to-save-fuel-without-sacrificing-speed/)). That guide teaches lifting at a reference board and braking later while keeping apex speed, shifting before the shift light, and fully lifting before braking. It also notes clutch-pull coasting is sim-only. An iRacing guide says fuel saving is pointless in races of about an hour or less and matters in longer ones, that stretching each stint by a lap can remove a stop, and that coasting before stops helps even without a full lap saved ([Coach Dave Academy](https://coachdaveacademy.com/tutorials/guide-to-strategy-in-iracing/)). Another says ERS maps include No Deploy, Qual, Attack, Balanced and Build, racing on Balanced and using Attack to pass (single source, extracted, verify in the car manual). Box This Lap says traction-control cuts over bumps and kerbs add up in fuel use (single source, unquantified) ([Box This Lap](https://boxthislap.org/iracing-gtp-endurance-guide-beginners/)). The LMU note that lifting early reduces regen and that a full battery loses regen help (search snippet; unverified) is a warning to check iRacing's behaviour, not a rule.

In traffic, an LMP1 driver stressed keeping momentum and committing rather than lifting mid-pass ([Autosport, 2011](https://www.autosport.com/wec/news/davidson-rules-make-traffic-harder-4446267/4446267/)). Summaries of other drivers say GT3 cars brake late and are better at slow-speed braking thanks to ABS, that Hypercars rejoining the line quickly under braking causes problems, and that most passes are on straights; these are search-summary level ([Motorsport Week](https://motorsportweek.com/?p=167996)). Sim coaches advise waiting for a better gap and using GTP acceleration after the apex ([Box This Lap](https://boxthislap.org/iracing-gtp-endurance-guide-beginners/)). Kubica framed endurance as minimising risk while still extracting performance ([PlanetF1](https://www.planetf1.com/news/robert-kubica-f1-sprinters-le-mans-marathons)); a Le Mans driver counters that modern Le Mans is six to eight full-speed stints ([24h-lemans.com](https://24h-lemans.com/en/news/when-the-24-hours-of-le-mans-turns-into-a-sprint-for-drivers-50588); summary). For a 2h40 race, the counterpoint is closer to the truth. Sim guidance: practise full race stints, use roughly 90 to 95% of potential, and expect lap 18 to differ from lap 2 ([Box This Lap](https://boxthislap.org/iracing-gtp-endurance-guide-beginners/); sim coach heuristic). Night references shift and judging distance to faster cars is harder ([24h-lemans.com](https://24h-lemans.com/en/news/out-of-the-darkness-and-into-the-light-night-driving-at-the-24-hours-of-le-mans-49490)), though a 2h40 IMSA race may not reach darkness.

**Suggestions (inference).** Express energy targets as "lift N metres earlier, brake later, same apex speed", the form the sim coach teaches, with the per-lap saving measured from the driver's own stints. Use fuel per lap as the proxy for energy in iRacing but flag the regen interaction. Do not port LMU penalties. Out-laps on cold GTP tyres warrant gentle, traction-limited throttle, extrapolated from one quote. Coach "pass on straights and exits, avoid braking-zone commitments against GT3, hold a predictable line when being passed". The traffic and pass-time costs are unquantified.

**Gaps.** No pro-published energy saved per lap; no engineer call wording for energy; nothing on shift points or line choice in modern GTP; no iRacing GTP fuel numbers; nothing on out-lap or handover technique, rain, or hydration; Ross Bentley's endurance material was not obtained.

## The chief engineer: titles vary and decision authority is a 2012 model

**Findings.** Titles are not standardised. Genesis lists a team principal, team manager, chief engineer, sporting director and technical director as separate posts ([The Race](https://www.the-race.com/endurance/touring-car-legend-gets-big-role-in-hyundais-new-wec-programme.md)); Porsche Penske added a lead race engineer who supports two car race engineers and oversees performance engineers, with the managing director citing added capacity ([Motorsport.com, Jan 2024](https://www.motorsport.com/imsa/news/porsche-bmw-technical-director-963-imsa-gtp/10566451/)). Fry held a technical director title at RLL and a lead race engineer title at Porsche, so title and seniority do not map one to one. A generic F1-oriented guide says a chief engineer's race-time job is reliability and operating limits and FIA technical contact (low confidence for endurance; snippet) ([motorsportexplained.com](https://motorsportexplained.com/type-of-f1-engineers-trackside-during-race-weekend)). Small customer teams use "technical director" for their lead engineer ([Sportscar365](https://sportscar365.com/imsa/iwsc/michelin-inside-the-role-engineers/)).

The one primary statement of authority is Toyota's Pascal Vasselon in 2012 (LMP1): immediate decisions belong to the car's race engineer, decisions with more time, such as tyres for the next stint, involve him, and higher-level decisions go through team management ([Autosport, 2012](https://www.autosport.com/wec/news/100341/q-and-a-with-pascal-vasselon)). In the same interview he describes the hybrid trade-off: stored energy can go to performance, lap-time target or fuel saving depending on the race scenario. Setup authority follows the driver with the most seat time in amateur-heavy crews (Braun and Lee, same Sportscar365 piece). A Toyota 2021 fuel-pressure fix chose a software workaround over a 45-minute garage stop (snippet) ([Motorsport.com](https://www.motorsport.com/wec/news/toyotas-fuel-issue-could-have-killed-le-mans-victory-hopes/6652119/)). Porsche's #6 race engineer is described as overseeing all activity on the car and managing run plans (snippet) ([Porsche Motorsport](https://racing.porsche.com/team-members/jean-philippe-sarrazin)). Team structure: Porsche Penske runs mirrored IMSA (Mooresville) and WEC (Mannheim) organisations ([Porsche newsroom PDF](https://newsroom.porsche.com/pdf/a4b7a6cb-73e8-49b8-a3b2-290df7c9a145); snippet), and Ganassi promoted a simulation engineer to race engineer ([Racer](https://racer.com/2021/12/14/ganassi-promotes-shepherd-to-race-engineer-for-imsa-team/); snippet).

**Suggestions (inference).** Map the race engineer to the single radio voice; route strategist and chief-engineer-style input through it. Classify messages by Vasselon's three tiers: immediate (assistant speaks now), planned-stint (assistant proposes and the driver agrees), strategic or sporting (shown on a screen or in the debrief, not spoken). The plausible split of technical director, chief or lead engineer and race engineer is inferred from titles. Nothing sourced says what chief engineers ignore in driver feedback.

**Gaps.** Who calls the stop in GTP/Hypercar (strategist, race engineer, team manager); how pit-wall disagreements are resolved; current-era (2020 to 2026) final-say statements; real org charts for Toyota, Ferrari AF Corse, Acura/MSR, BMW/RLL, Cadillac; engineers per car; briefing and meeting rhythm; the hardest parts of the job; books and podcasts.

## Driver feedback: the vocabulary is standard, the fix table is not sourced

**Findings.** Drivers call understeer "push" or "tight" and oversteer "loose", split by entry, mid-corner and exit ([Racer racerpedia](https://racer.com/racerpedia/racing-101/what-are-oversteer-and-understeer); search summary). One sim vendor puts the ideal at mild entry understeer and just enough exit oversteer for direction, while an F1 forum view prefers slight exit understeer for traction ([SimXPro](https://simxpro.com/en-us/blogs/guides/understeer-vs-oversteer-how-to-diagnose-it-and-fix-it-in-one-corner); [f1technical forum](https://www.f1technical.net/forum/viewtopic.php?p=158953)); the sources disagree, so the assistant should not hard-code an ideal. Entry understeer often starts with braking and turn-in timing, exit oversteer with throttle timing and steering rate, so driver cause comes before car cause (SimXPro). Too much front brake bias stops the car turning in, and abrupt brake release while steering lets the rear keep rotating ([Speed Secrets Q&A](https://speedsecrets.com/q-how-can-i-reduce-the-amount-of-understeer-i-experience-with-my-driving-technique-or-cars-setup/); summary). A club slide deck adds that too stiff a front roll bar makes understeer grow through the corner ([PCA Car Balance](https://nnjr-pca.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Car-Balance.pdf); summary). An IndyCar engineer says a request for front grip must say whether low or high speed ([Motorsport.com](https://www.motorsport.com/indycar/news/ripple-effect-engineering-Arrow-SPM/4480010/); summary).

On structure, a driver-coach page advises tying feedback to specific corners and conditions, writing an organised list, giving on-track feedback so changes happen at the next stop, and using a full debrief when time allows ([Tom Long Racing](https://tomlongracing.com/2020/03/05/answering-your-questions-turning-feedback-into-adjustments/)). Its example mappings (rear unsettled in fast corners, lower rear or more wing; understeer, lower or soften front) are single-source and generic. Good engineers do not want drivers dictating changes, per the Winding Road / Ross Bentley piece (snippet only; page returned 403) ([Winding Road](https://windingroad.com/articles/features/speed-secrets-getting-the-most-out-of-driver-feedback/)). An iRacing GTP guide says feedback is useful when repeatable (same phase in the same corner type), that parameters should be changed one at a time, and that "car feels fine but laps don't come" points to technique ([Box This Lap](https://boxthislap.org/iracing-gtp-endurance-guide-beginners/)).

For in-car levers, all direction rules come from sim guides. Bias forward gives braking stability, rearward more entry rotation; front locking first means bias rearward, rear first means forward; bias is driver-specific, so teammates' numbers should not be copied; about 44% was cited as one driver's value (reference only) and migration examples run around -3 to -5 (illustrative). A softer front ARB gives rotation, stiffer favours stability; traction control should be the lowest intervention the driver can run consistently, conservative early, looser when warm and tighter with wear (all [Box This Lap](https://boxthislap.org/iracing-gtp-endurance-guide-beginners/), single guide). An LMU LMGT3 guide has bias forward on cold tyres and rearward when warm (different car and sim) ([SimRacingCockpit](https://simracingcockpit.gg/le-mans-ultimate-how-to-drive-the-lmgt3-and-hypercar/)). Forum posts say bias matters little once ABS is active (anecdotal) ([GTPlanet](https://www.gtplanet.net/forum/threads/brake-balance.372919/)). Setup requests go through the engineer, and in-car changes go through wheel controls ([24h-lemans.com](https://24h-lemans.com/en/news/from-chalk-to-talk-how-driver-pit-communications-have-evolved-at-the-24-hours-of-le-mans-57062)). That page also notes engineers give per-turn consistency feedback, and radio lets drivers warn the next driver. A Porsche director said two-driver crews mean less setup compromise ([The Race](https://www.the-race.com/endurance/the-dilemma-surrounding-wec-driver-crews/)).

**Suggestions (inference, unsourced; verify before shipping).** The symptom table is mine: entry understeer with front locking, bias rearward; entry instability under braking, bias forward; low-speed mid-corner push, softer front or stiffer rear ARB; high-speed push, aero rather than ARB (garage-only); exit oversteer, more TC or the ARB change above; exit wheelspin, more TC. Step sizes of about 0.5 to 1.0% bias, one ARB step and one TC step per request are also mine, as are my definitions of terms like snap, lazy and platform. Parse (symptom, phase, corner or speed class, severity) and ask one clarifying question if one is missing; accept words like "bit" and "lot" and numeric scales and normalise them. Test driver versus car by repeatability across laps and corners and by telemetry inputs (steering, brake release, throttle) before proposing a change; make one reversible change, then confirm over the next laps. Ask "different from the start of the stint?", since fuel burn and tyre wear shift balance in directions no pro source confirms. Store per-driver preferences separately from the shared setup, and at handover report current in-car settings, tyre state and the last few notable behaviours. For talking to the driver: one actionable point per message, on straights, short imperative plus one reason, positive framing, retract before contradicting, and detail deferred to a debrief; these combine the radio sources above with my own judgement.

## Conclusion

The sources support a clear design stance but not a data set. Practitioners converge on discipline in communication and on the idea that the engineer is a single calm voice, which is directly implementable; they converge on lift and coast as the energy lever; and they show strategies as named options judged against cautions and traffic. What they do not provide is the quantitative layer, so the assistant's value will come from measuring pit loss, fuel per lap, tyre fall-off and variance in the user's own iRacing sessions, with the sourced pattern names and the Vasselon urgency tiers as scaffolding. Anything resembling a number from this report (the 920/902/915/907/903 MJ figures, the 255.6 kWh and 500 kW claim, the 40 s refill, the 80 L in 40 s, the 1.45 L and 0.7 s per lap, the 44% and -3 to -5 bias examples) is single-source, real-world or from a different sim, and belongs in documentation as context only.

The sharpest open risk is the setup-assistance layer: every direction rule for bias, ARB and TC in the notes comes from sim guides or my own reasoning, and one source says ABS blunts bias effects. The most valuable follow-up research is therefore targeted: the iRacing GTP car manuals and forums for tank, energy and regen behaviour, the Ross Bentley and Racecar Engineering material that could not be retrieved, and a direct read of the IMSA sporting regulations for minimum fill and work-area rules.
